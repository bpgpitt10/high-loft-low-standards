Player by player

Net order. Gross context.

Roast first. Then enough data to explain what actually happened.

1

MashedBROtato NET -4 · GROSS +6

The irons showed up; the scorecard brought a flamethrower.

MashedBROtato won the net race at -4 and the gross race at +6 because this was a spectacularly high-functioning mess. The ball-striking did the heavy lifting: 6.14 strokes gained on approach and 10.82 tee-to-green, with an eagle and two birdies keeping four bogeys and three doubles from turning the card into a crime scene. Even the ugly hole was only a seven, and the par-five recovery eventually produced a net birdie. After prior events ranging from -5.87 to +7.24 total strokes gained, this is a volatile profile suddenly pointed in the right direction. Three 3-putts remain the one loose floorboard in an otherwise championship-grade round.

2

CRANK NET +1 · GROSS +16

The short game performed miracles on a course that wanted a confession.

CRANK’s net +1 was built on survival, not conventional golf. A +16 gross round still became the closest threat because the short game gained 6.99 and putting added 2.29, while only two greens in regulation made every clean-up shot matter. The par-five disaster was the round in miniature: deep trouble early, sand repeatedly afterward, then a nine-footer to keep the damage from becoming terminal. CRANK’s two prior net wins made another serious net run unsurprising, and the current result keeps that profile intact even while the approach game cratered by 4.56 strokes. This was ugly golf with an elite emergency response team.

3

DREADFUL NET +4 · GROSS +22

The approach game was sharp until the wedges started seeking asylum.

DREADFUL finished net +4 with a round split cleanly between promise and punishment. The driver gained 2.28 and approach gained 4.33, producing 6 greens in regulation and enough tee-to-green gain to suggest the long game was ready to behave. Then short game dropped 6.01 and putting lost 2.85, turning six bogeys and four doubles into a gross +22. The par-five catastrophe was the loudest evidence: a string of bunker recoveries that made the hole progressively less solved before a late hole-out. After three prior appearances carrying a -5.75 SG average, this was a meaningful tee-to-green improvement, buried beneath the same short-game volatility.

4

Dan94 NET +8 · GROSS +8

The long game was excellent; the scoring department misplaced the memo.

Dan94 produced the second-best gross round at +8, but the net result was +8 as well, leaving a lot of raw-golf quality without the leaderboard reward it deserved. He gained 5.27 off the tee and 5.97 on approach, hit 9 greens, and posted 11.75 total strokes gained—exactly the kind of ball-striking that should make a simulator screen nervous. The catch was a brutal par-four at the end of the card, where repeated wedge recoveries turned a manageable hole into an eight. His prior profile already showed 13.35 tee-to-green SG per appearance, so this round confirms the elite ball-striking more than it changes anything. The putter was merely negative, but the short game could not fully mop up the mess.

5

Leroy4522 NET +12 · GROSS +24

The putter saw 37 chances and still wanted more evidence.

Leroy4522’s net +12 was a grind from the wrong side of the golf equation. The tee game gained 2.0, but approach lost 3.09 and putting lost 3.74, while four greens in regulation and 37 putts made every missed target feel like a recurring subscription. The worst hole was a par-three that wandered through sand and fairway before finally arriving 22 feet away, an ambitious route for a hole that only asked for one tee shot. His prior three-event profile already carried -4.89 tee-to-green and -3.42 total SG per appearance, so this round fits the established struggle rather than inventing a new one. Seven fairways were not enough to rescue the scoring.

6

KeithK75 NET +12 · GROSS +12

The approach play was clean; the rest of the card kept setting off alarms.

KeithK75 had the third-best gross score at +12 and gained 7.75 total strokes, with 5.31 coming on approach and another 1.59 off the tee. That is real ball-striking, even if eight bogeys and three doubles made the scorecard look like it had been edited by a committee. The par-four at the turn captured the problem: two bunker visits, a fairway detour, and a six that never felt close to being a six. After three prior appearances averaging 12.91 total SG with wins in both net and gross competition, this was a clear step down in result despite solid tee-to-green work. Three 3-putts and a negative putting number supplied the missing chaos.

7

HamSammich NET +15 · GROSS +30

The driver found trouble, and the short game filed a complaint.

The clubs found plenty of golf course, just not enough useful golf.

8

IUDoc92 NET +20 · GROSS +39
