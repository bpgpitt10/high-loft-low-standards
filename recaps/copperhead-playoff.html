Player by player

Net order. Gross context.

Roast first. Then enough data to explain what actually happened.

1

KeithK75 NET +1 · GROSS +1

Copperhead’s fairway-finding tax collector

KeithK75 won this tournament the old-fashioned simulator way: by being brutally good at nearly everything. The +1 net and +1 gross sweep was backed by 18.63 total strokes gained, with approach at 6.28 and tee play at 4.96. He also made two birdies, hit nine greens, and avoided every double bogey and three-putt. The only real blemish was the ugly escape on 16, where a zero-yard second shot briefly made the playoff favorite look mortal before the next swing found semirough and the green. With five prior starts and a 1.6 average gross finish, this was less a surprise than a reminder that Keith’s raw game has been living near the top of the board.

2

HamSammich NET +2 · GROSS +17

The putter carrying a bag full of subpoenas

HamSammich finished one shot behind Keith in net, and the round was a strange split-screen: the scoring held together while the raw ball-striking did not. He gained 2.77 putting strokes and 1.76 off the tee, but lost 1.24 on approach, hit only four greens, and posted a 45.6 GIR proximity. The +17 gross result says the round was held together by clutch net value and a putter willing to clean up the wreckage. On the fifth, a par five became an eight after repeated rough visits, exactly the sort of hole that explains both the ugly gross number and the impressive net survival. After three prior starts, this is a meaningful bounce from his recent volatility, but the irons still need adult supervision.

3

IUDoc92 NET +7 · GROSS +24

Short game by way of the scenic route

IUDoc92 earned third in net at +7 despite a gross +24, because the round’s best work happened after the ball had already made several poor life choices. Approach gained 2.10, but short game lost 4.76 and putting lost 0.87; three greens in regulation made every missed target expensive. The par-three collapse on 8 was the clearest snapshot, with multiple rough recoveries before a six. Still, he found two net birdies on 6 and 10, and the net result was better than his gross finish would suggest. Across five prior starts, the profile remains firmly mixed-to-rough: a 6.8 average net finish against an 8.0 average gross finish, with total strokes gained at -12.1 per appearance.

4

DREADFUL NET +9 · GROSS +27

A three-putt enthusiast with a short-game alibi

DREADFUL’s +9 net finish was more respectable than the +27 gross score, but the raw round was a tug-of-war nobody won. He hit just three greens and gained nothing meaningful across the course: tee, approach, short game, and putting all finished negative, for -7.37 total strokes gained. The putting damage showed up everywhere, including four three-putts and a 35-putt day. On 11, a par five turned into a nine after the ball bounced through rough and pinestraw, then the 32-footer started a three-putt encore. Five prior starts support the broader read: the net profile is competitive, with a 4.8 average net finish, but the gross game keeps leaking strokes in every department.

5

CRANK NET +10 · GROSS +22

Short-game wizard trapped behind one green

CRANK’s +10 net round was built almost entirely on survival skills. The short game gained 3.28, but approach lost 3.85, only one green was hit, and the raw score climbed to +22. That is a lot of damage for one patch of grass to absorb, even when the recovery game is doing unpaid overtime. The eighth shot on 12 was the predictable ending to a hole that wandered through rough and pinestraw before finally producing a 17-footer. Yet the recent profile still says net contender: three net wins in five prior starts and a 1.6 average net finish. The gross result is the warning label, while the short game remains the reason the net card refuses to die.

6

Dan94 NET +11 · GROSS +11

Big tee game, small appetite for fairways

Dan94 was the raw-golf contradiction of the day: second in gross at +11, but sixth in net. The underlying numbers explain the split. He gained 6.43 off the tee, 3.10 around the green, and 8.63 total, yet hit only three greens and made eight bogeys. On 7, the tee shot found sand, the next attempt found more sand, and the ball moved one yard before the hole demanded a long putt. That is how a strong tee-to-green day can still turn into a scoreboard shrug. His five-start history remains excellent in raw terms, with a 1.8 average gross finish and 12.7 total strokes gained per appearance; the net result simply did not reward the fireworks this time.

7

MashedBROtato NET +13 · GROSS +25

The short game filed for immediate dismissal

MashedBROtato’s +13 net round unraveled around the green, where he lost six strokes and never found a fairway. The tee and approach numbers were positive enough—1.81 and 0.90—but four greens, 34 putts, and two three-putts left too much cleanup for one golfer. The eighth was the round in miniature: a par three became an eight after a series of sand-adjacent non-escapes, followed by two putts that kept the hole expanding. That is a sharp drop from his five-start profile, which carries a 5.4 average net finish and a positive 0.86 total strokes gained per appearance. The useful part is that the ball-striking did not completely disappear; the short game took the whole afternoon hostage.

8

Leroy4522 NET +17 · GROSS +29

Every recovery shot needed its own recovery shot

Leroy4522 finished at +17 net and +29 gross, with the round sinking under -7.72 tee-to-green strokes gained. Only two greens were hit, and the approach game lost 3.13 while the tee game gave away another 3.73. The worst hole on 12 supplied the full diagnosis: trouble off the tee, more trouble on the recovery, then sand and a long march toward the green. There were no birdies to offset four bogeys and four doubles, so every missed target arrived with a surcharge. His five-start history shows a familiar raw-game problem, especially in approach at -4.67 strokes gained per appearance. The net profile has had better moments, but Copperhead offered no hiding place for the misses.
