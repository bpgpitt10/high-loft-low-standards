Tournament in 30 seconds

Keith survived Ham by one.

Net was a race. Gross was a demolition. Both stories matter, but the league trophy starts with net.

The actual league finish was tight at the top: Keith won net at +1, Ham finished one shot back at +2, IUDoc92 took third at +7, and DREADFUL grabbed fourth at +9. Ham's 15 handicap turned a +17 gross round into a genuine challenge, while Keith had no handicap cushion at all. That is the fun of the format: the best golfer still has to beat everybody's number, not just everybody's raw score.

Underneath that, Keith was clearly the best golfer on the property. He gained +18.63 Total SG, hit 9 greens, needed 27 putts, made zero doubles, and beat Dan by ten gross. Dan still produced the week's best tee number at +6.43 SG Tee, but three GIR and -2.12 on approach left him second gross and only sixth net. CRANK landed fifth net at +10, Mashed seventh at +13, and Leroy brought up the rear at +17. So yes, Keith dominated the golf — but Ham made him actually win the league.

Player by player

Net order. Gross context.

The league result determines the order. The golf underneath explains how everybody got there.

1

KeithK75 NET +1 · GROSS +1

Keith had to beat Ham by one for the league win, which is the part that matters first. The obnoxious part is that he also didn't really have a weak department to hide: +4.96 off the tee, +6.28 on approach, +4.49 around the green and +2.91 putting for +18.63 total. Nine GIR, 27 putts, zero doubles, no handicap cushion. He closed with three straight bogeys and still hung on to the net title by one while winning gross by ten. Ham made the tournament competitive; Keith still supplied the best actual golf by a mile.

2

HamSammich NET +2 · GROSS +17

Ham was the guy who made this a league tournament instead of a Keith exhibition. The gross 88 was third-best in the field, and a 15 handicap converted it into +2 net — one lonely shot behind Keith. He made zero birdies and three doubles, including the spectacular triple on 5 after piping the tee ball down the fairway, but 28 putts and +2.77 SG Putting kept enough wreckage off the card to stay in the hunt. It was also his best gross finish of the season. There is legitimate golf in here; it just keeps getting interrupted by holes where six consecutive shots appear to have never met each other before.

3

IUDoc92 NET +7 · GROSS +24

IU quietly took third in the league competition, which gets lost completely if you stare only at the gross board. His 17 handicap brought +24 gross down to +7 net, and the interesting golf underneath was +2.10 SG Approach — easily the healthiest part of the bag. Unfortunately the short game immediately responded by losing -4.76, which is a fairly hostile way to reward improved iron play. He hit six greens, made two 3-putts and spent too many missed-green holes needing multiple attempts just to create a putt. Third net, with enough approach improvement to hint at what would show up later at Sage Valley.

4

DREADFUL NET +9 · GROSS +27

DREADFUL finished fourth net despite a gross card that contained almost every known form of damage: only three GIR, 35 putts, four 3-putts and negative strokes gained in every category. The 18 handicap did what handicaps are supposed to do and kept the league result respectable while the golf underneath remained deeply unstable. Hole 11 was the masterpiece — nine on a par 5, including four putts after finally reaching the green. He also birdied 15 and holed a 14-yard shot to save par on 12, because DREADFUL golf refuses to commit to one genre for more than a few minutes.

5

CRANK NET +10 · GROSS +22

Seven fairways. One green. +3.28 short game. This is the CRANK experience distilled into laboratory-grade concentrate. A 12 handicap brought the gross +22 down to fifth-place net +10, but nearly every approach created another recovery assignment, and his 30 putts are less “good putting” than the inevitable outcome of chipping onto nearly every green from somewhere nearby. Hole 12 delivered the signature explosion, with the shot log even dropping an event between rough and pinestraw before the scorecard finally settles on eight. CRANK keeps proving there are many ways to play golf, and hitting greens is merely one optional school of thought.

6

Dan94 NET +11 · GROSS +11

Dan was the second-best gross golfer and only sixth in the league competition because scratch players get exactly what they shoot. His driver was still the most violent weapon on the property: +6.43 SG Tee, 275 yards on fairway-finding drives and a 296-yard longest. The rest of the bag simply declined to participate at the same level. He hit only three greens, lost -2.12 on approach and made back-to-back doubles on 6 and 7. That's the frustrating version of Dan — enough tee advantage to make the course look short, then enough iron nonsense to make it long again.

7

04
State of the league

Ham made Keith earn it.

The league story is not “Keith won by ten.” That's the gross story. The league story is that Keith won net by one over Ham, with IU third and DREADFUL fourth. Keith remains clearly the strongest actual golfer, but the handicap format did exactly what it is supposed to do: gave everybody a different path into the tournament and made the best golfer sweat for the result that counts.

Underneath that, Keith and Dan still occupy the top gross tier, Ham just posted his best gross finish of the season, IU showed encouraging approach play, and CRANK remained exactly CRANK — fairways, missed greens, short-game emergency services. Net keeps the league fun. Gross tells us who actually played the best. We are keeping both stories, in that order.
