Tournament in 30 seconds

Keith won this everywhere.

The score was +1. The underlying golf was even more one-sided.

Keith gained +18.63 total strokes, including +15.72 tee-to-green. He hit 9 greens, needed 27 putts, made zero doubles, and beat Dan by ten. There was no magic category carrying a mediocre round; he was just good at basically every part of golf at the same time, which is an obnoxious way to make a league event boring.

Dan still produced the week's best tee number at +6.43 SG Tee, but only hit three greens and lost -2.12 on approach. Ham's +17 gross was not pretty, but a 15 handicap turned it into +2 net and second place in the spreadsheet tournament. Everyone else finished at least 21 shots over par. This was less a chase and more a prolonged confirmation that Keith had already left.

03
Player by player

Everybody gets a paragraph.

More room here. People came to read about themselves, not admire our restraint.

1

KeithK75 +1 · net +1

Keith didn't really have a weak department to hide. He gained +4.96 off the tee, +6.28 on approach, +4.49 around the green and +2.91 putting for a ridiculous +18.63 total. Nine GIR doesn't even sound dominant until you notice he needed only 27 putts and made zero doubles, while everybody chasing him was finding increasingly creative ways to turn bogey into something worse. He played the front in 34, then closed with three straight bogeys and still won by ten. When your “collapse” is three bogeys after you've already buried the field, the league has a Keith problem.

2

Dan94 +11 · net +11

Dan's driver was still the most violent weapon on the property: +6.43 SG Tee, 275 yards on the drives that found fairway and a 296-yard longest. The rest of the bag simply declined to participate at the same level. He hit only three greens, lost -2.12 on approach and made back-to-back doubles on 6 and 7. That's the frustrating version of Dan — enough tee advantage to make the course look short, then enough iron nonsense to make it long again. He still finished second gross because the floor is absurdly high when you're starting every decent hole from fifty yards closer than everyone else.

3

HamSammich +17 · net +2

Ham produced the kind of round handicaps were invented to make socially acceptable. He made zero birdies and three doubles, including the spectacular triple on 5 after piping the tee ball down the fairway, but 28 putts and +2.77 SG Putting stopped the card from becoming a total landfill fire. The gross 88 was third-best in the field and the 15 handicap converted it to +2 net, good for second. More importantly, this was his best gross finish of the season. There is legitimate golf in here; it just keeps getting interrupted by holes where six consecutive shots appear to have never met each other before.

4

CRANK +22 · net +10

Seven fairways. One green. +3.28 short game. This is the CRANK experience distilled into laboratory-grade concentrate. The tee ball was actually functional enough, but almost every approach created another recovery assignment, and his 30 putts are less “good putting” than the inevitable outcome of chipping onto nearly every green from somewhere nearby. Hole 12 delivered the signature explosion: the shot log even drops an event between rough and pinestraw before the scorecard finally settles on eight. CRANK keeps proving there are many ways to play golf, and hitting greens is merely one optional school of thought.

5

IUDoc92 +24 · net +7

IU's approach number was the surprise: +2.10 SG Approach was genuinely useful and easily the healthiest part of the bag. Unfortunately the short game immediately responded by losing -4.76, which is a fairly hostile way to reward improved iron play. He hit six greens, made two 3-putts and spent too many missed-green holes needing multiple attempts just to create a putt. The triple on 2 is the perfect example: from 134 yards out, four more shots were required to reach the surface. There were signs of the better ball-striking that would show up later at Sage Valley, but Copperhead still demanded a lot of cleanup work.

6

MashedBROtato +25 · net +13

Mashed somehow gained +1.81 off the tee while hitting zero fairways, which should probably trigger a congressional hearing into what “tee success” means. The real destruction came elsewhere: -6.00 short game, 34 putts and the now-historic eight on the par-3 8th. That hole alone included three recorded bunker attempts, one of them going zero yards, followed by a three-putt after escape. This is why Mashed remains the league's most entertaining player: there are enough legitimately good shots to suggest a score is about to happen, immediately followed by a sequence nobody would believe if the shot log weren't sitting right there.

7

DREADFUL +27 · net +9

DREADFUL posted negative strokes gained in every major category and needed 35 putts, including four 3-putts. Then, because this game enjoys making analysis look stupid, he also holed from 14 yards for par on 12 and made the field's lone birdie on 15. Hole 11 was the other side of that coin: five shots to reach the green and four more to finish, including a four-putt sequence from 24 feet. The entire round was a fight between “this is getting ugly” and random moments of competence appearing from behind a bush. The username continues to function less as branding and more as informed consent.

8

Leroy4522 +29 · net +17

Leroy finished at +29 with -9.37 Total SG, and Copperhead made sure the damage was distributed broadly enough that there was no single department to fire. The worst moment was a nine on the par-4 12th. SGT's shot log skips shot numbers 3 and 5, so the scorecard gets the final word and we are not inventing whatever horrors went missing. What remains visible is pinestraw, rough, sand and finally a green nine shots later. It's a fitting snapshot of the round: not one spectacular failure so much as a long sequence of small problems refusing to stop introducing themselves.

04
State of the league

Keith is becoming a problem.

Another gross win pushed Keith further into the season's top tier. Dan still owns the league's biggest tee advantage, but Copperhead showed exactly why Keith's complete game is harder to beat: when the course gets ugly, he simply has fewer departments available to betray him.

Ham's third-place gross finish was the interesting move underneath them. CRANK remained exactly CRANK — fairways, missed greens, short-game emergency services — while Mashed and DREADFUL continued their respective campaigns to prove that a golf score can contain multiple genres.
