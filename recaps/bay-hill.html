Player by player

Net order. Gross context.

Roast first. Then enough data to explain what actually happened.

1

CRANK NET -7 · GROSS +5

Won the net fight with a short-game alibi.

CRANK’s round was a net survival story, not a display of pristine ball-striking. The top net score came at -7 despite a +5 gross card and negative tee-to-green SG of -1.37, with the short game and putter doing the heavy lifting at 2.68 and 2.83 respectively. Five greens in regulation and four bogeys left plenty of wreckage, but 24 putts, no three-putts, and two birdies kept the damage contained. The defining mess was the seven-shot net six on the eighth, where rough-bound recoveries nearly swallowed the advantage. Then came the useful stuff: a net eagle on the sixth and a made 16-footer on 17. First eligible appearance, first net win, and absolutely no reason to pretend it was pretty.

2

TEV_11 NET -7 · GROSS +3

Bombed it beautifully, then made the greens a group project.

TEV_11 tied the net mark at -7 on the strength of a genuinely strong tee game: 3.83 strokes gained off the tee and 3.35 tee-to-green. Twelve greens and five birdies show the underlying golf had plenty of horsepower, even if eight bogeys and three three-putts kept trying to turn the card into a complaint form. The putting number finished barely positive at 0.11, which is remarkable considering the 33 putts and the 58-footer that needed a second attempt before finally becoming a seven-footer. The cleanest evidence came on the par fives, where net eagles arrived on both four and 16. With no prior starts, this is a strong opening statement: launch it, find enough greens, and let Bay Hill sort out the rest.

3

MashedBROtato NET -7 · GROSS +1

A birdie machine with zero fairways and two plot twists.

MashedBROtato tied the net lead at -7 while producing the best all-around underlying profile among the front three, with 7.24 total strokes gained and 6.43 tee-to-green. The card was not exactly a controlled laboratory experiment: zero fairways, four bogeys, and two doubles sat beside an eagle, five birdies, and six-for-six sand saves. That combination explains the round better than any single stat. The short game kept rescuing the misses, while the approach play added 2.45 SG and the putter stayed useful at 0.81. Even the ugly sixth hole became a gross three and net two, because apparently the proper response to missing fairways is to start making eagles. First eligible appearance, and already a compelling blend of violence and repair work.

4

KeithK75 NET -5 · GROSS -5

Played Bay Hill like the fairways owed him money.

KeithK75 produced the tournament’s best raw round at -5 gross, and the numbers back up every bit of the claim: 14 greens, 10 birdies, and 11.46 total strokes gained. Approach play led the assault at 4.76 SG, while 9.20 tee-to-green made the round look less like a hot streak and more like a sustained beating. The net score also landed at -5, good enough to stay in the front group but not enough to win the net pileup. The lone crater was the seven on the third, where rough turned into more rough, then sand, before a two-footer ended the negotiations. He answered with gross birdies everywhere, including a clean four on both fourth and sixth. Raw-golf champion; handicaps remain a separate legal system.

The approach game filed for leave and never came back.

There were flashes of competent golf on the par fives, including net threes on 12 and 16, but the rest of the round spent too much time asking the approach shot to explain itself.
