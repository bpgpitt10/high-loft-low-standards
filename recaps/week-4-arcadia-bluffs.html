Net order. Gross context.

Player by player

1

Dan94 NET -3 · GROSS -4

Mostly merciless. Golf tried a late jab and got bodied.

Dan94 took the net crown and also owned gross, which is what happens when a dude shows up with the entire bag working. The ball-striking was the big flex: 14 greens, the best tee-to-green line in the field, and the kind of approach work that keeps you from living in the weeds. He even stapled down the ugly spots with no three-putts and a single bogey, then cashed in an eagle and three birdies like the scorecard owed him rent. The weird part is this almost looks normal now: after 11 prior starts, his profile is just one long argument that he usually plays better than the rest of the room, and this one was the cleanest version yet.

2

MattF NET E · GROSS -1

The irons showed up, the tee game showed up in a cab.

MattF was the only guy who really stayed on Dan94’s doorstep in net, and the story was familiar: enough iron play to hang around, not enough off the tee to stop the round from feeling slightly held together with tape. The approach work was the engine, but the driver was spraying like it had somewhere else to be, and the lone three-putt plus a pair of bogeyish nicks kept the round from getting spicy. He’s now got three strong recent looks at this thing, and the shape is pretty clear: when the putter cooperates he can hang with anybody, but the tee ball still likes to wander off and smoke behind the clubhouse.

3

BigTime NET +1 · GROSS +2

The scorecard had highlights; the front nine had the hangover.

BigTime finished right in the thick of the net fight, but this was one of those rounds where the raw pieces looked better than the final taste. The irons did their job, the putter was actually a help, and the tee game was respectable enough to keep the whole mess from turning into a full-scale excavation. The problem was the usual stupid tax: a double mixed into the card and just enough extra bogey sludge to keep the round from cashing the way the ball-striking suggested. Over four completed starts, that’s still a player who can show quality golf without fully shaking the short-game-and-big-number nonsense that keeps sneaking into the margin.

4

KeithK75 NET +4 · GROSS +1

The approach play was sharp; the rest of the round brought the receipts.

KeithK75’s gross finish says the irons were there, and the stats back that up: this was a decent tee-to-green day that got mugged by the scoring holes around it. He hit plenty of greens and was excellent with approach, but the round leaked through the short game and the one double-sized blemish on the card. That’s the whole story with him right now: in 11 starts he’s been all over the place, usually competent enough to make you think something cleaner is coming, then one ugly patch drags the whole thing back into the mud. This one was basically that in a nutshell.

5

IUDoc92 NET +5 · GROSS +15

A fairway-friendly disaster with a putter that kept the lid on the coffin.

IUDoc92’s net result was the least awful thing about a round that was otherwise a full-contact event with par. The tee ball was actually serviceable, which only made the rest of it look more irritating: very few greens, too many bogeys, and a couple of doubles and worse to make sure nothing got rescued by accident. He did at least avoid the truly catastrophic meltdown and picked up one birdie, but this is still the same broad shape we’ve seen over 11 starts — the profile is volatile, with enough decent tee work to tease competence and enough missed shots around the greens to turn the whole day into a long apology.

6

Leroy4522 NET +7 · GROSS +16

The tee ball was fine; the rest of the spreadsheet was a cry for help.

Leroy4522 never really found enough stuff to make the round competitive, and the card shows why: one lonely birdie, a pile of bogeys, and too many chances that died before they ever became a story. The driver wasn’t a total disaster, but the approach game and the short-game support just weren’t there often enough to keep the round from bleeding out. Over 11 starts, the trend is still the same ugly one — plenty of bad holes, not enough scoring, and a profile that can occasionally flash a decent tee ball while the rest of the round sags like a folding chair.

7

MashedBROtato NET +9 · GROSS +15

The tee ball showed up, the rest of the bag called in sick.

This was a classic MashedBROtato round: enough ball-striking to keep the corpse from floating, enough short-game leakage to make the score look like it had been attacked with a pipe wrench. He still got home in better net shape than his gross line suggests, but the round was dragged around by the ugly stuff between the tee shot and the putter, not the driver. The one bright spot was the iron work, which at least gave him a few looks and a couple of usable holes, but the price tag was too many bogeys, a double, and the kind of around-the-green pain that turns a decent starting point into a slog. At this point the profile is pretty established: better than the early-round train wrecks, still perfectly capable of making a par 3 feel like a hostile takeover.

After only a couple prior starts, the signal is still cautious, but the shape is clear enough: when the ball-striking goes sideways, the rest of the round doesn’t have the firepower to cover for it.

9
