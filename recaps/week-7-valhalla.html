Player by player

1

MattF NET +6 · GROSS +6

Driver said yes, irons said maybe, and the putter filed paperwork later.

MattF grabbed the net win with a round that was way more survival than artistry, but the ugly bits were contained just enough to hold off the field. The long game was the engine, especially off the tee, while the short game bailed him out when the approach play got flaky and the card tried to wobble. He didn’t pepper greens all day, but the scramble work was loud enough to matter, and a clean finish on 18 helped seal it. This is pretty on-brand over six starts now: multiple top-end results, a history of living near the front, and enough all-around firepower that even a mediocre ball-striking day can still cash a check.

2

KeithK75 NET +7 · GROSS +4

The tee ball was dialed, but the card still found one hole to mug him.

KeithK75 brought the rawest punch of the week and still got edged out in net, which is a pretty annoying way to win gross and miss the big trophy by one slot. The driver was the story, with tee play carrying the day while the irons stayed merely fine and the putting was good enough to keep the card from detonating. He leaked one ugly hole into the round, but otherwise this was the kind of front-loaded damage that gives everybody else a headache. With 14 prior starts, this fits the profile: he lives near the top, wins gross often, and keeps showing up with enough tee-to-green muscle to make the rest of the field work for it.

3

Dan94 NET +8 · GROSS +6

Bombs off the tee, then the wedges showed up wearing clown shoes.

Dan94 was the definition of feast-or-ferky golf: enough tee power and short-game help to stay relevant, but too little consistency on the scoring clubs to turn the round into anything cleaner. The tee game was loud, the putter was steady, and the approach work was just a little too sideways to let the round breathe. That ugly par 5 early on was the kind of hole that forces a scorecard to start negotiating with itself, and even a decent closing stretch couldn’t fully erase it. Over 14 starts, this is still a volatile but dangerous profile — plenty of ceiling, plenty of mess, and enough previous wins that nobody should pretend the upside isn’t real.

4

Leroy4522 NET +9 · GROSS +19

Every shot looked like it had to pass a background check before it left the clubface.

Leroy4522 dragged a rough ball-striking day across the finish line by keeping the putter and recovery game from fully collapsing. That’s the whole story: the tee ball was passable, but the approach game was a brick wall and the greens were basically a rumor. Still, he avoided the total clown-car outcome by grinding through the mess, and the scorecard settled into something merely ugly instead of catastrophic. The larger trend is pretty clear over 14 starts: this is still a low-floor player, but the recent results show enough occasional damage control that the round doesn’t always spiral all the way into the ditch.

5

IUDoc92 NET +9 · GROSS +18

The irons showed up; the putter showed up late to the meeting and drunk.

IUDoc92 basically lived on approach play and still had to drag the rest of the bag across the finish line with a crowbar. The irons were the one real bright spot, and the short game did enough to keep the round from becoming a total car wreck, but the putter was a straight-up hostage-taking situation. That monster blow-up on 6 was the signature scar, the kind of hole that makes an otherwise respectable tee-to-green day look like it got hit by a truck. Still, this is a better shape than his usual profile over 14 starts: more competitive tee-to-green work, less total implosion, and just enough scoring competence to keep him from free-falling.

6

Jakobm90 NET +16 · GROSS +26

The tee ball went on a bender and the rest of the round had to call it an Uber.

Jakobm90’s round was what happens when the driver goes missing and the scorecard has to file a police report. The tee game was the main disaster, and everything else spent the day trying to mop up after it — approach play was actually serviceable, the short game wasn’t hopeless, and the putter did what it could while staring into the abyss. The problem was the parade of ugly holes that started early and never really stopped, including that par 5 massacre that set the tone immediately. Over five prior starts, the trend is still pretty blunt: this player can survive when the tee ball is merely bad, but when it goes full nuclear, the rest of the bag doesn’t have enough fire extinguishers.

7

MashedBROtato NET +22 · GROSS +27

The putter tried to save the round; the short game dragged it behind the shed.

This was a familiar kind of beatdown with one weird bright spot: MashedBROtato finished seventh in both nets and gross, but the round never stopped bleeding where it mattered. The tee game was actually the least offensive part, and the putter chipped in enough to keep the corpse twitching, but the short game absolutely torched the scorecard. Four sand chances, zero saves, four three-putts, and an 11-shot disaster on 15 are how you turn a round into a tax audit. That’s the story again for him: the upside is real enough to show up, but the bottom half of the bag keeps kicking the chair out from under it.

8

derekldavis NET +25 · GROSS +41

Driver was a liability, and the rest of the bag had to fight uphill in mud.

derekldavis came in eighth in both nets and gross, and the round looked exactly like the numbers suggest: the tee ball was a crime scene, the putter was the only thing that showed up with a pulse, and everything else spent the day looking for a way to survive. The longer view is brutal but consistent: this is now a five-start profile of getting carved up off the tee and not nearly enough from the rest of the bag to patch it together. The putter kept this from being even uglier, which is basically handing out paper towels in a house fire.
