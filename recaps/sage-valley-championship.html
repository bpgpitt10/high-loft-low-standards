Tournament in 30 seconds

IU picked a hell of a time to show up.

Net determines the tournament. Gross and SGT strokes gained explain the golf underneath it.

IU won Sage Valley by five net shots. His -7 was comfortably clear of Mashed, Ham and Dan, all at -2. DREADFUL was fifth at even and CRANK sixth at +1. This was not a tiebreaker or a spreadsheet squeaker; IU simply posted the number everybody else had to stare at.

The raw-golf story was Dan: -2 gross, +11.65 Total SG, +5.97 tee, +3.94 approach, 12 GIR and 29 putts. Keith was second gross at +3. IU's +10 gross was only fourth, but +2.35 approach and 33 putts were enough to give his 17 handicap something useful to work with. The best gross golfer and the tournament winner were very different people, exactly as intended.

Player by player

Net order. Gross context.

Roast first. Then enough data to explain what actually happened.

1

IUDoc92 NET -7 · GROSS +10

The irons finally showed up and the handicap immediately turned into a weapon of mass accounting.

IU gained +2.35 approach and +1.01 putting, hit seven GIR and posted +10 gross. Tee and short game remained negative, so this was not suddenly flawless golf; it was simply enough competent golf in the right places for a 17 handicap to turn lethal.

2

MashedBROtato NET -2 · GROSS +10

Nine greens, 33 putts, and a closing triple because peace was never an option.

Mashed was near neutral everywhere: +1.21 approach, +0.29 putting, -0.16 tee. Nine GIR gave the round structure, while the seven on 18 provided the mandatory chaos tax. +10 gross became a useful -2 net.

3

HamSammich NET -2 · GROSS +13

A seven on a par 3 and still a useful round. Handicap golf is a beautiful scam.

Ham gained +1.06 short game but lost -2.27 putting and -1.63 tee. Seven GIR and 30 putts were enough to keep +13 gross from becoming a disaster, and a 15 handicap did the rest.

4

Dan94 NET -2 · GROSS -2

Hit fairway and green, then needed four putting actions. Even the bombers eventually meet a flat surface.

Dan gained +5.97 tee and +3.94 approach, hit 12 GIR and finished at +11.65 Total SG. The four-putt-style sequence on 17 was the obvious blemish. Otherwise this was exactly what the gross winner is supposed to look like.

5

DREADFUL NET E · GROSS +18

Forty putts. The putter spent the entire championship submitting expense reports.

DREADFUL hit eight GIR but needed 40 putts and lost -6.02 short game. Putting SG is recorded near zero, which is suspicious enough not to build the story around; the raw 40-putt count is plenty damning by itself.

6

CRANK NET +1 · GROSS +13

The tee ball remained hostile, but the approaches briefly filed for independence.

CRANK lost -5.24 tee but gained +2.56 approach. Five GIR and 33 putts kept the score at +13 gross. A 12 handicap turned that into +1 net, close enough to be relevant but not enough to survive IU's sudden competence.

7

KeithK75 NET +3 · GROSS +3

Ten greens, positive everywhere, and still somehow made the card look merely normal.

Keith gained +2.92 tee, +2.71 approach and +1.38 putting for +6.65 Total SG. Ten GIR and 30 putts produced +3 gross. It was solid golf; it just lacked the usual Keith-level refusal to make mistakes.

8

Leroy4522 NET +5 · GROSS +17

The rough came in regular and deep. Leroy ordered both.

Leroy lost -4.03 approach and -2.58 tee, hit five GIR and needed 31 putts. The closing triple was the loudest problem, but the approach number explains why the whole round kept needing repairs.

04
State of the league

IU won the tournament. Dan won the golf.

Sage Valley is the cleanest argument for keeping net first and gross visible. IU's -7 was the tournament result and it was not particularly close. Dan's -2 gross was clearly the best raw golf. Both are true, neither needs to pretend to be the other, and the page is more fun because everybody gets a real lane into the story.
